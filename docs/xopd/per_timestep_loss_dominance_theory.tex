\title{\textbf{Why late timesteps dominate the XOPD per-step loss}\\[0.3em]
\large A flow-matching account of the \texttt{d\_k} blow-up, artifact vs.\ signal,
and the \texttt{xopd\_dk\_space} reweighting}
\author{Flow Factory}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Theory companion to the empirical observation
\texttt{docs/xopd/progress/2026-07-12-per-timestep-dk-dominance.md} (last quarter of steps
$\approx 87\%$ of $\sum_k \dk$, final step $\approx 46\%$ at \texttt{mean\_tail}); the implemented remedy is
\texttt{train.xopd\_dk\_space="x0"} (clean-latent loss, \S\ref{sec:impl}). Relevant
code: \texttt{compute\_per\_step\_kl} in
\texttt{src/flow\_factory/trainers/xopd/common.py}, called by
\texttt{\_precompute\_d\_per\_timestep} / \texttt{\_optimize\_train\_pass} in
\texttt{src/flow\_factory/trainers/xopd/trainer.py}.

This note defines four implemented per-step knobs ($L_{xt}$, $L_v$, $L_{x_0}$,
$L_{x_0}^{\mathrm{norm}}$) plus the Gaussian transition KL $L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ (DanceOPD theory,
\S\ref{ssec:danceopd-kl}) --- formulas in \S\ref{sec:loss-defs} --- their exact conversion on the
FLUX.2-klein 28-step grid, and why the default $L_{xt}$ (config \texttt{"xt"}) dominates late
timesteps. Companion data: \S\ref{sec:schedule}--\S\ref{sec:empirical}.
\end{abstract}

%% ============================================================
\section*{Five per-step losses at a glance}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Five per-step losses at a glance}
\label{sec:loss-defs}
%% ============================================================

At every discrete step~$k$, student and teacher are evaluated on the \emph{same} on-policy state
$x_t$; only their velocity predictions $v_s$ (student) and $v_t$ (teacher) differ. Let
$\sigma=t/1000$ be the FM noise fraction and
$\dt=\sigma^{\mathrm{sched}}_{k+1}-\sigma^{\mathrm{sched}}_k<0$ the schedule step. The five
losses compared throughout this note are:
\begin{align}
L_{xt}
  &= \norm{\mu_s-\mu_t}^2
  &&\text{(default; \texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="xt"})},
\label{eq:def-Lxt}\\[0.35em]
L_v
  &= \norm{v_s-v_t}^2
  &&\text{(\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="v"})},
\label{eq:def-Lv}\\[0.35em]
L_{x_0}
  &= \norm{z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}}^2
  &&\text{(\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0"})},
\label{eq:def-Lx0}\\[0.35em]
L_{x_0}^{\mathrm{norm}}
  &= \frac{\norm{z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}}^2}
          {\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}|\big)+\varepsilon}
  = \frac{L_{x_0}}
          {\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}|\big)+\varepsilon}
  &&\text{(\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0\_norm"})},
\label{eq:def-Lx0norm}\\[0.35em]
L_{\mathrm{KL}}
  &= \KL\!\bigl(p_m(\cdot\mid x_t)\,\big\|\,p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_t)\bigr)
  = \frac{\dt^2}{2\sigma^2}\,L_v
  = \frac{L_{xt}}{2\sigma^2}
  &&\text{(DanceOPD transition KL)}.
\label{eq:def-Lkl}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Definitions via the velocity bridge $v_s,v_t$.}
The transition means and clean latents are \emph{deterministic functions} of the shared state
$x_t$ and each model's velocity:
\begin{align}
\mu_s &= x_t + v_s\,\dt,
\qquad
\mu_t = x_t + v_t\,\dt
&&\text{(ODE one-step means)},
\label{eq:def-mu}\\
z_{0,s} &= x_t - \sigma\,v_s,
\qquad
z_{0,t} = x_t - \sigma\,v_t
&&\text{(recovered clean latents)}.
\label{eq:def-z0}
\end{align}
Because $x_t$ is shared, it cancels in every difference, so $v_s-v_t$ is the single bridge:
\begin{align}
\mu_s-\mu_t &= \dt\,(v_s-v_t),
\label{eq:bridge-mu}\\
z_{0,s}-z_{0,t} &= -\sigma\,(v_s-v_t).
\label{eq:bridge-z0}
\end{align}
Squaring both sides immediately yields the ODE identities
$L_{xt}=\dt^2 L_v$, $L_{x_0}=\sigma^2 L_v$, and hence
$L_{\mathrm{KL}}=L_{xt}/(2\sigma^2)=\dt^2 L_v/(2\sigma^2)$
(\S\ref{sec:three-losses}). The self-normalized variant $L_{x_0}^{\mathrm{norm}}$
\eqref{eq:def-Lx0norm} divides $L_{x_0}$ by a \emph{detached} per-sample mean-abs scale
($\mathrm{sg}=$ stop-gradient; $\varepsilon>0$ floors the denominator), so it is
scale-invariant and \emph{not} a fixed schedule reweighting of $L_v$
(\S\ref{sec:selfnorm}). The first four losses are implemented knobs in
\texttt{compute\_per\_step\_kl}; $L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is the theoretical same-variance Gaussian
transition KL (\S\ref{ssec:danceopd-kl}).

%% ============================================================
\section{Three per-step MSE losses and their exact conversion}
\label{sec:three-losses}
%% ============================================================

XOPD compares student and teacher on the \emph{same} on-policy rollout state $x_t$ at step
index~$k$; only their predictions differ. Under rectified-flow interpolation
($x_t=(1-\sigma)z_0+\sigma\varepsilon$, $\sigma=t/1000$) and ODE Euler stepping
($\mu=x_t+v\,\dt$, $\dt=\sigma^{\mathrm{sched}}_{k+1}-\sigma^{\mathrm{sched}}_k<0$), there are
three equivalent fixed MSE targets plus one adaptive self-normalized variant (config
\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space}, default \texttt{"xt"}; definitions
\eqref{eq:def-Lxt}--\eqref{eq:def-Lx0norm}):

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{knob} & \textbf{loss} & \textbf{formula} & \textbf{note} \\
\midrule
\texttt{xt} & $L_{xt}$ & $\norm{\mu_s-\mu_t}^2$ \eqref{eq:def-Lxt} & transition mean / next latent (default) \\
\texttt{v} & $L_v$ & $\norm{v_s-v_t}^2$ \eqref{eq:def-Lv} & raw velocity MSE; ODE-only \\
\texttt{x0} & $L_{x_0}$ & $\norm{z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}}^2$ \eqref{eq:def-Lx0} & clean-latent MSE; ODE-only \\
\texttt{x0\_norm} & $L_{x_0}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ & $L_{x_0}/(\mathrm{sg}(\mathrm{mean}|z_{0,s}-z_{0,t}|)+\varepsilon)$ \eqref{eq:def-Lx0norm} & DiffusionNFT/DMD self-norm; ODE-only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Ratio.} $\mathrm{MSE}(v):\mathrm{MSE}(xt):\mathrm{MSE}(x0) = 1:\dt^2:\sigma^2$.

Here $\mu$ is the one-step transition mean ($x_{t+\dt}$ under ODE), $v$ the model velocity
(\texttt{noise\_pred}), and $z_0=x_t-\sigma v$ the analytically recovered clean latent. Because
$x_t$ cancels:
\begin{align}
\mu_s-\mu_t &= \dt\,(v_s-v_t),
\label{eq:mu-v}\\
z_{0,s}-z_{0,t} &= -\sigma\,(v_s-v_t),
\label{eq:x0-v}\\
\Rightarrow\quad
L_{xt} &= \dt^2\,L_v,
\label{eq:mu-v-sq}\\
L_{x_0} &= \sigma^2\,L_v,
\label{eq:x0-v-sq}\\
L_{x_0} &= \Big(\tfrac{\sigma}{\dt}\Big)^2 L_{xt},
\label{eq:x0-mu}\\
L_v &= \Big(\tfrac{\dt}{\sigma}\Big)^2 L_{x_0}.
\label{eq:v-x0}
\end{align}
All four identities are \emph{exact} on the discrete ODE grid (no approximation). The conversion
factors depend \emph{only} on $(\sigma_k,\dt_k)$ at step~$k$; they do not require equal errors
across steps --- they convert \emph{the same} underlying $(v_s-v_t)$ disagreement between spaces.

\paragraph{Conversion matrix (multiply to go from column loss to row loss).}
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l|ccc@{}}
 & $\to L_{xt}$ & $\to L_v$ & $\to L_{x_0}$ \\
\midrule
from $L_{xt}$ & $1$ & $\dt^{-2}$ & $(\sigma/\dt)^2$ \\
from $L_v$ & $\dt^2$ & $1$ & $\sigma^2$ \\
from $L_{x_0}$ & $(\dt/\sigma)^2$ & $\sigma^{-2}$ & $1$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{What each loss implicitly weights (if you sum $\sum_k L_{\{\cdot\},k}$ with coefficient~$1$).}
Fix the \emph{underlying} velocity disagreement $L_{v,k}$ at step~$k$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{$L_{xt}$ (default):} step~$k$ contributes $\dt_k^2 L_{v,k}$ --- late steps have larger
$\lvert\dt_k\rvert$ and dominate (\S\ref{sec:schedule}, col.~``\%$L_{xt}$'').
\item \textbf{$L_v$ (\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="v"}):} step~$k$ contributes $L_{v,k}$ directly --- the
native FM velocity target; under the fixed 28-step grid the per-step $\dt_k$ vary only modestly so
this is close to uniform (\S\ref{sec:empirical}).
\item \textbf{$L_{x_0}$ (\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0"}):} step~$k$ contributes $\sigma_k^2 L_{v,k}$ ---
early high-noise steps ($\sigma\approx1$) dominate (col.~``pct $L_{x_0}$'').
\end{itemize}
Alternatively, fix $L_{x_0,k}$ (equal clean-latent disagreement): $L_{xt,k}=(\dt_k/\sigma_k)^2
L_{x_0,k}$, which is the ``$(\dt/\sigma)^2$ blow-up'' narrative (\S\ref{sec:causes}).

\subsection{Fourth form: Gaussian transition KL (DanceOPD / DiffusionOPD theory)}
\label{ssec:danceopd-kl}

The three knobs above are \emph{unweighted} per-step MSE targets. A separate, widely used objective
is the \textbf{same-variance Gaussian transition KL} between teacher and student one-step
distributions (definition~\eqref{eq:def-Lkl}). Under a local model
$p(\cdot\mid x_t)=\N(\mu,\,\sigma_t^2 I)$ with shared covariance~$\sigma_t^2$ (DanceOPD
Appendix~7.1; DiffusionOPD pathwise term), and $\mu=x_t+v\,\dt$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
L_{\mathrm{KL},k}
=\KL\!\bigl(p_m(\cdot\mid x_t)\,\big\|\,p_\theta(\cdot\mid x_t)\bigr)
=\frac{\dt_k^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\,\norm{v_s-v_t}^2
=\frac{L_{xt,k}}{2\sigma_k^2}.
}
\label{eq:danceopd-kl}
\end{equation}
This is \textbf{not} equal to any of $\{L_{xt},L_v,L_{x_0}\}$ with unit coefficient; it is a
\emph{fourth} reweighting of the same underlying velocity gap. Exact conversions (same
$(v_s-v_t)$ at step~$k$):
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l|cccc@{}}
 & $\to L_{xt}$ & $\to L_v$ & $\to L_{x_0}$ & $\to L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ \\
\midrule
from $L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ & $2\sigma^2$ & $2\sigma^2/\dt^2$ & $2\sigma^4/\dt^2$ & $1$ \\
from $L_{xt}$ & $1$ & $\dt^{-2}$ & $(\sigma/\dt)^2$ & $(2\sigma^2)^{-1}$ \\
from $L_v$ & $\dt^2$ & $1$ & $\sigma^2$ & $\dt^2/(2\sigma^2)$ \\
from $L_{x_0}$ & $(\dt/\sigma)^2$ & $\sigma^{-2}$ & $1$ & $\dt^2/(2\sigma^4)$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{Implicit weight on $L_v$.} Summing $\sum_k L_{\mathrm{KL},k}$ with coefficient~$1$ applies
$w_k^{\mathrm{KL}}=\dt_k^2/(2\sigma_k^2)$ to the underlying $L_{v,k}$ --- \emph{between} the
$L_{xt}$ weight ($\dt_k^2$) and the $L_{x_0}$ weight ($\sigma_k^2$): early high-noise steps are
down-weighted by $1/\sigma_k^2$ relative to $L_{xt}$, but late steps with small~$\sigma_k$ still
\emph{amplify} $1/\sigma_k^2$ enough that $L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is \textbf{more} late-heavy than
$L_{xt}$ under flat~$L_v$ (Figure~\ref{fig:theoretical-shares}: $\mathbf{77\%}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{41\%}$
on \texttt{ti=27}).

\paragraph{DanceOPD vs.\ our \texttt{normalize\_d\_k}.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{DanceOPD training default} is \emph{plain} $L_v=\norm{v_s-v_t}^2$ at a single
semantic-side low-$t$ query --- \eqref{eq:danceopd-kl} is the \emph{theoretical} KL identity; their
ablations report plain MSE beats KL-$\bar\sigma^2$ / Min-SNR reweightings
(\texttt{docs/mof/danceopd\_comparison.md}).
\item \textbf{Our \texttt{normalize\_d\_k=true}} (only in \texttt{space="xt"}) divides transition
mean MSE by $2\bar\sigma_k^2=2\,\mathrm{std\_dev}_t^2\,\lvert\dt_k\rvert$ (SDE Appendix~B
$\bar\sigma$), \emph{not} by $2\sigma_k^2$ with FM fraction~$\sigma_k=t_k/1000$. Under ODE
($\mathrm{std\_dev}_t\approx0$) it falls back to plain $L_{xt}$. So \eqref{eq:danceopd-kl} is
\textbf{conceptually related} to normalized transition KL but \textbf{not implemented} as a separate
knob; it differs from $L_{\mathrm{KL}}$ unless one identifies $\bar\sigma$ with~$\sigma_k$ and uses
SDE dynamics.
\item \textbf{Ratio to $L_{xt}$:} $L_{\mathrm{KL},k}/L_{xt,k}=1/(2\sigma_k^2)$. On the FLUX.2-klein
grid this \emph{increases} late-step share vs.\ plain $L_{xt}$ (77\% vs.\ 41\% on \texttt{ti=27}
under flat~$L_v$), because $\sigma_k\to0$ faster than $\dt_k^2$ shrinks toward the end.
\end{itemize}

%% ============================================================
\section{The key identity (legacy shorthand)}
\label{sec:identity}
%% ============================================================

With \texttt{normalize\_d\_k=false}, the logged \texttt{train/d\_k/\{ti\}} is $L_{xt}$ (not $L_v$).
Combining \eqref{eq:mu-v-sq}--\eqref{eq:v-x0}:
\[
L_{xt} = \dt^2 L_v = \Big(\tfrac{\dt}{\sigma}\Big)^2 L_{x_0}.
\]
So the default loss is clean-latent disagreement multiplied by the implicit per-step weight
$(\dt/\sigma)^2$ (\S\ref{sec:three-losses}). The $x$-space knob sets $L_{x_0}$ directly and removes
that factor (\S\ref{sec:impl}).

\paragraph{Notation at step index \texttt{ti}.}

precision.

\paragraph{Reading the numbers.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{At \texttt{ti=27}, all three losses coincide in scale.} $\lvert\dt_{27}\rvert\approx
\sigma_{27}$, so $\dt_{27}^2=\sigma_{27}^2\approx0.037$ and $L_{xt}=L_{x_0}=L_v$ for the \emph{same}
velocity gap.
\item \textbf{Default $L_{xt}$ dominance $\approx$ flat $L_v$.} If $\norm{v_s-v_t}^2$ were identical
at every step, $\sum_k L_{xt,k}$ puts $\mathbf{41\%}$ on \texttt{ti=27} (col.~``\%$L_{xt}$'').
The mixed XOPD run observes $\mathbf{46\%}$ on \texttt{ti=27} at \texttt{mean\_tail} --- the logged curve matches
\emph{roughly constant velocity error} amplified only by $\dt_k^2$, not the stronger
$(\dt_k/\sigma_k)^2$ factor (which would give $\mathbf{77\%}$ if $L_{x_0,k}$ were constant).
\item \textbf{$L_{x_0}$ up-weights early steps.} With flat $L_v$, high-noise steps each take
$\sim5\%$ of $\sum L_{x_0}$ while \texttt{ti=27} takes only $\mathbf{0.19\%}$. Setting
\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0"} switches to this allocation.
\item \textbf{Cross-step ratios (same $L_v$).} $L_{xt,27}/L_{xt,13}=(\dt_{27}/\dt_{13})^2
\approx\mathbf{148\times}$; $L_{x_0,0}/L_{x_0,27}=\sigma_0^2/\sigma_{27}^2\approx\mathbf{27\times}$.
\item \textbf{``Late step'' $\neq$ $\sigma\to0$.} The last rollout step has $t=192$ ($\sigma=0.19$),
not $t\approx0$. Dominance comes from the discrete grid's large $\lvert\dt_k\rvert$ at the end.
\end{itemize}

%% ============================================================
\section{Two causes of the dominance: separate the artifact from the signal}
\label{sec:causes}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Cause A --- $\dt_k^2$ amplification in $L_{xt}$ (a parameterization artifact)}
\label{ssec:causeA}

The default loss is $L_{xt}=\dt^2 L_v$ (\eqref{eq:mu-v-sq}), not velocity MSE. Summing
$\sum_k L_{xt,k}$ with unit weight therefore applies a per-step factor $\dt_k^2$ to the underlying
velocity gap. Late rollout steps have larger $\lvert\dt_k\rvert$ (Table~\ref{tab:klein28-summary}),
so \textbf{$L_{xt}$ over-represents late steps even if $L_v$ were flat}.

Quantitative check on the FLUX.2-klein 28-step grid (\S\ref{sec:schedule}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item If $L_{v,k}\equiv$ const: $\sum_k L_{xt,k}$ puts $\mathbf{41\%}$ on \texttt{ti=27}
(col.~``\%$L_{xt}$''). The mixed XOPD run observes $\mathbf{46\%}$ on \texttt{ti=27} for logged
$L_{xt}$ --- the curve matches \emph{roughly constant velocity error} $\times\,\dt_k^2$, not a
separate ``mystery'' effect.
\item If instead $L_{x_0,k}\equiv$ const: $L_{xt,k}=(\dt_k/\sigma_k)^2 L_{x_0,k}$ would put
$\mathbf{77\%}$ on \texttt{ti=27}. The observed $\mathbf{50\times}$ ratio $L_{\mu,27}/L_{\mu,13}$
is \emph{between} the flat-$L_v$ prediction ($\sim148\times$ from $\dt$ ratio alone, before
$L_v$ shrinkage) and the flat-$L_{x_0}$ prediction ($\sim3100\times$), because both the metric
\emph{and} the actual $L_v$ vary with~$k$.
\item Switching to $L_{x_0}$ (\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0"}) replaces the $\dt_k^2$ weighting with
$\sigma_k^2$ (col.~``\%$L_{x_0}$''), up-weighting high-noise structure steps.
\end{itemize}
This parallels diffusion training, where $\varepsilon$-prediction MSE needs SNR reweighting to avoid
low-noise dominance; here the analogue of ``native'' FM regression is $L_v$, not $L_{xt}$.

\subsection{Cause B --- genuine difficulty near the data manifold (a real signal)}
\label{ssec:causeB}

Near clean, the posterior $\E[z_0\mid x_t]$ sharpens, the velocity field's spatial Jacobian
(Lipschitz constant) grows $\sim 1/\sigma$, and the target encodes sharp, high-frequency, multimodal
detail. Under \texttt{normalize\_d\_k=false} the latent scale is also largest at clean. This part of
the late-step cost is \textbf{real} and tied to final-image quality.

\subsection{The U-shape}
\label{ssec:ushape}

The observation doc's minimum at ti$\approx12$--$13$ and mild high-noise bump at ti$=0$ follow from
$L_{xt}=\dt^2 L_v$ with $L_v$ largest at high noise: early steps have small $\dt^2$ but large $L_v$;
late steps have large $\dt^2$ but shrinking $L_v$; the minimum is where these cross.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!5,colframe=red!50!black,title=\textbf{Corollary: \texttt{normalize\_d\_k=true} makes it worse, not better}]
\texttt{normalize\_d\_k=true} divides by $2\bar\sigma^2=2\,\mathrm{std\_dev}_t^2\,\lvert\dt\rvert$,
and $\bar\sigma\to0$ toward clean, stacking a \emph{second} amplification on top of $1/\sigma^2$.
This is why transition-KL objectives (DanceGRPO/DDPO-style) intrinsically favor the low-noise
region. \textbf{To equalize timesteps you must go the opposite way.}
\end{tcolorbox}

%% ============================================================
\section{XOPD-specific reading (large teacher)}
\label{sec:xopd}
%% ============================================================

``XOPD benefits more because the teacher is larger'' is correct, but the benefit lives in
\textbf{Cause B}, not A:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The 32B$\to$4B teacher's advantage (fine detail, texture, aesthetics) concentrates in the
\emph{low-noise} steps, where on-policy $x_t$ is already near the student's final sample --- the most
valuable place for a big teacher to ``polish''.
\item Structural/semantic correctness (geneval count/position, OCR) is decided at \emph{high-noise}
steps.
\end{itemize}
So ``large teacher'' does \textbf{not} imply ``train only late steps''. It implies ``the late-step
Cause-B component is worth keeping --- but do not mistake the Cause-A amplification for that value.''

\section{Adaptive self-normalized reweighting (DiffusionNFT / DMD) for the OPD $d_k$}
\label{sec:selfnorm}

\paragraph{Empirical motivation.} With \texttt{xopd\_dk\_space}$\in\{$\texttt{v},\texttt{x0}$\}$ (the two
that win, and to within noise are equal) the per-step magnitude $\dk$ is \emph{tilted the other way}
from the \texttt{xt} case of \S\ref{sec:empirical}: it is \textbf{larger at early (high-noise) steps and
smaller at late (clean) steps}. A single optimizer update over the $28$-step trajectory is therefore
dominated by the early steps, and the clean end (which carries fine detail) is under-trained. We ask
whether a \emph{per-step reweighting} that equalizes the step contributions helps.

\paragraph{DiffusionNFT's weighting.} DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al., 2025, NVlabs/DiffusionNFT) replaces
the manual $w(t)$ of Eq.~(1) with a \emph{self-normalized $\bm{x}_0$ regression} borrowed from DMD
(Yin et al., 2024):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nft}
  w(t)\,\norm{\bm{v}_\theta(\bm{x}_t,\bm{c},t)-\bm{v}}_2^2
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \frac{\norm{\bm{x}_\theta(\bm{x}_t,\bm{c},t)-\bm{x}_0}_2^2}
       {\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|\,\bm{x}_\theta(\bm{x}_t,\bm{c},t)-\bm{x}_0\,|\big)},
\end{equation}
with $\bm{x}_\theta=\bm{x}_t-t\,\bm{v}_\theta$ (rectified flow) and $\mathrm{sg}$ the stop-gradient. The
denominator is a \emph{detached} per-sample scale (mean absolute $\bm{x}_0$ error), so \eqref{eq:nft} is
a \textbf{scale-invariant} regression: its gradient is the ordinary $\bm{x}_0$-MSE gradient divided by
its own error magnitude, giving every $(\text{sample},t)$ a gradient of comparable RMS.

\paragraph{Implied time weight.} Under rectified flow the target is $\bm{x}_0=\bm{x}_t-t\bm{v}$, so
$\bm{x}_\theta-\bm{x}_0=-t(\bm{v}_\theta-\bm{v})$ and $\mathrm{mean}|\bm{x}_\theta-\bm{x}_0|=t\,\mathrm{mean}|\bm{v}_\theta-\bm{v}|$.
Substituting into \eqref{eq:nft},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weff}
  \eqref{eq:nft}=\underbrace{\frac{t}{\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|\bm{v}_\theta-\bm{v}|\big)}}_{\;=:~w_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\;}\,\norm{\bm{v}_\theta-\bm{v}}_2^2 .
\end{equation}
Two facts follow. (i) The scheme is \textbf{not} a naive equalizer: it keeps an explicit $t$ factor that
\emph{up-weights high noise} --- exactly the direction the paper reports is stable, whereas the inverse
$w(t)=1-t$ (up-weighting the clean end) \emph{collapses}. (ii) On top of the $t$ tilt it divides by the
\emph{measured} per-step error scale, i.e.\ it adapts to the very early-large/late-small profile we observe
and flattens the \emph{effective gradient} without hard-flipping the tilt.

\paragraph{Transfer to the OPD $d_k$ (teacher target).} Our $d_k$ is a \emph{distillation} MSE whose
target is the frozen teacher's prediction, not data. The construction transfers verbatim by replacing
$\bm{x}_0$ with the teacher clean-latent $\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{t}}$ and $\bm{x}_\theta$ with the student
$\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{s}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opdnorm}
  \dk^{\mathrm{norm}}
  \;=\;
  L_{x_0}^{\mathrm{norm}}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\norm{\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{t}}}_2^2}
       {\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|\,\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{t}}\,|\big)+\varepsilon}
  \;=\;
  \frac{t}{\mathrm{sg}\!\big(\mathrm{mean}\,|\bm{v}_{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{v}_{\mathrm{t}}|\big)+\varepsilon}\,
  \norm{\bm{v}_{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{v}_{\mathrm{t}}}_2^2 ,
\end{equation}
(definition~\eqref{eq:def-Lx0norm}; config \texttt{xopd\_dk\_space="x0\_norm"}),
applied independently at each trajectory step $k$ (with $t=t_k$). The two forms are identical because
$\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{x}_0^{\mathrm{t}}=-t(\bm{v}_{\mathrm{s}}-\bm{v}_{\mathrm{t}})$; the second is the
cheaper drop-in for a $\bm{v}$-space $d_k$. The floor $\varepsilon>0$ guards the denominator (the
teacher-student gap $\to 0$ near convergence, so $1/c_k\to\infty$ without it).

\paragraph{Why this may beat the fixed \texttt{normalize\_d\_k}.} \texttt{normalize\_d\_k} divides $d_k$ by
$2\bar\sigma^2$ --- a \emph{fixed}, schedule-only reweighting equivalent to a particular $t$-profile. Eq.
\eqref{eq:opdnorm} instead divides by the \emph{measured} per-step teacher-student error, so it auto-tunes
to the empirical early-large/late-small shape rather than a prior. It is the data-adaptive analogue of the
loss-space identity $\mathrm{MSE}(v):\mathrm{MSE}(x_t):\mathrm{MSE}(x_0)=1:\dt^2:\sigma^2$: instead of
choosing a space to reweight, it normalizes each step by its own realized magnitude.

\paragraph{Predictions and risks.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{If} the early-step dominance was mostly a scale artifact, \eqref{eq:opdnorm} should lift
clean-end fidelity (detail-sensitive eval: OCR text, geneval attributes) at equal compute, mirroring the
$25\times$ efficiency DiffusionNFT reports.
\item \textbf{Risk (collapse).} Do \emph{not} implement a hard equalizer or inverse tilt ($w\propto 1-t$ or
$w\propto 1/\|\dk\|$ that cancels the $t$ factor): the paper shows this collapses. Keep the $t$ factor of
\eqref{eq:weff} (i.e.\ normalize in $\bm{x}_0$ space, not raw $\bm{v}$ space) so high noise stays up-weighted.
\item \textbf{Stop-gradient is load-bearing}: without $\mathrm{sg}$ the denominator enters the loss surface
and the objective degenerates. \item \textbf{Convergence}: as the teacher-student gap shrinks the effective
weight grows; monitor for late-training instability and rely on $\varepsilon$ + \texttt{max\_grad\_norm}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation sketch.} Add \texttt{xopd\_dk\_weighting}$\in\{$\texttt{none},
\texttt{selfnorm\_x0}$\}$; when \texttt{selfnorm\_x0}, compute the per-step $\bm{x}_0$ (or $\bm{v}$) student
$-$ teacher error, divide the per-step $d_k$ by $\mathrm{sg}(\mathrm{mean}|\cdot|)+\varepsilon$ per
\eqref{eq:opdnorm}, and keep it orthogonal to \texttt{xopd\_dk\_space} (it \emph{subsumes} the fixed
\texttt{normalize\_d\_k}). Ablate against \texttt{none} on the $v$-MSE and $x_0$-MSE arms.
