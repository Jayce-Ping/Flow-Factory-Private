# Why 9B→4B blurs while 32B→4B improves

## Setup

The analysis uses 128 fixed prompts (32 each from GenEval, OCR, PickScore and
Hard-T2I), a 28-step 512px ODE trajectory generated by the base 4B student, and
teacher queries on the exact same `(x_t, t, prompt)` states.

```text
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/teacher_velocity_x0_analysis.pdf
```

Confidence intervals below use a cluster bootstrap over 128 prompts. All 28
steps of one prompt stay in the same bootstrap cluster.

## Main result

### 1. The harmful 9B correction is smaller, not larger

Across 3,584 prompt-step observations:

- `RMS(v9 − v4) = 0.121`, 95% CI `[0.118, 0.123]`.
- `RMS(v32 − v4) = 0.551`, 95% CI `[0.536, 0.565]`.

The 32B correction is about 4.55× larger. Therefore 9B→4B failure is not
explained by an oversized teacher step. A scalar trust radius would suppress
the useful 32B direction more strongly than the harmful 9B direction.

### 2. The two teachers point in different directions

The mean cosine between the two corrections

```text
Δ9  = v9  − v4
Δ32 = v32 − v4
```

is only `0.250`, 95% CI `[0.237, 0.263]`. It is approximately `0.03` at the
first steps and rises to `0.53` at the final step. The models disagree most at
high noise. The 9B teacher is not a lower-magnitude version of the 32B teacher.

### 3. The one-step 9B x0 correction cancels detail

For rectified flow,

```text
x0 = x_t − sigma * v,  sigma = timestep / 1000.
```

We compare spatial gradients and Laplacians of the 4B x0 prediction with the
teacher-induced correction.

- Gradient cosine, 9B: `−0.168`, CI `[−0.177, −0.159]`.
- Gradient cosine, 32B: `+0.191`, CI `[+0.181, +0.201]`.
- Laplacian cosine, 9B: `−0.186`, CI `[−0.195, −0.177]`.
- Laplacian cosine, 32B: `+0.161`, CI `[+0.150, +0.173]`.

Negative cosine means the correction cancels existing spatial changes. Positive
cosine means it reinforces them. This sign reversal is the clearest latent-space
signature of the observed blur.

The direct measures agree:

- 9B changes x0 total-variation RMS by `−0.00150`,
  CI `[−0.00219, −0.00079]`.
- 32B changes it by `+0.13642`,
  CI `[+0.12867, +0.14457]`.
- 9B has no significant global high-frequency power increase:
  `+0.00012`, CI `[−0.00031, +0.00056]`.
- 32B adds high-frequency power:
  `+0.00895`, CI `[+0.00716, +0.01079]`.

The gradient/Laplacian signs reproduce across GenEval, OCR, Hard-T2I and
PickScore. PickScore has nearly zero 9B TV change, but its 9B gradient and
Laplacian alignments remain negative.

### 4. The scheduler exposes two different questions

The clean-prediction gap is `sigma * |Δv|`, so it is largest at high noise and
shrinks near the end. The direct transition-mean loss used by historical XOPD
is `|dt| * |Δv|`; it grows toward the final steps under this schedule.

These should not be conflated:

- high-noise x0/detail metrics explain what semantic/detail direction a teacher
  proposes;
- late-step transition metrics explain where the direct `xt` objective spends
  gradient.

A per-step gate should inspect both quantities.

## Hidden-state observations

Raw activation RMS is architecture-specific and should not be compared without
normalization. The useful dimensionless quantity is each block's
`output_RMS / input_RMS`, together with token-geometry CKA.

The 64-D CountSketch CKA shows:

- double-stream token geometry has moderate cross-model agreement;
- 4B and 9B are generally closer than 4B and 32B in early blocks;
- 32B single-stream geometry is strongly different from Klein 4B/9B.

This is secondary evidence. CKA uses a fixed sketch rather than full hidden
states and the three models have different widths. It should guide layer
selection, not be treated as a causal blur metric.

Dimensionless block gain adds two observations. The first and last double-stream
blocks amplify RMS strongly in all three models, while most middle blocks stay
near one. The 9B and 32B single-stream blocks are almost norm-preserving
(`output_RMS/input_RMS ≈ 1`); 4B has stronger mid-stack contraction and
late-stack expansion. These scale patterns distinguish architectures but do not
separate the harmful and useful teacher as cleanly as the x0 detail-direction
metrics.

## Metrics to use going forward

### Field geometry

1. `RMS(vT − v4)` and relative RMS.
2. `cos(v9 − v4, v32 − v4)`.
3. Per-token correction cosine and its lower quantiles.
4. Teacher correction projected onto the actual checkpoint update.

### One-step prediction

1. `RMS(x0_T − x0_4)`.
2. Gradient and Laplacian cosine between `x0_4` and `x0_T − x0_4`.
3. Total variation, Laplacian RMS and high-frequency FFT power.
4. VAE-decoded one-step x0 sharpness, faithfulness and reward.

### Scheduler-aware objective

1. Velocity gap `|Δv|`.
2. Clean gap `sigma * |Δv|`.
3. Transition gap `|dt| * |Δv|`.
4. Cumulative per-step contribution under the exact training loss.

### Internal representations

1. Block output/input RMS gain.
2. Token-geometry linear CKA at matched relative depth.
3. Attention q/k/v norm, entropy and effective rank.
4. MLP/attention residual contribution ratio.
5. Source- and timestep-conditioned outlier quantiles.

## Causal follow-ups

1. Decode `x0_4`, `x0_9`, and `x0_32` at matched steps for the 16 full-capture
   prompts. Confirm that latent anti-detail alignment predicts visual blur.
2. Form `v_lambda = v4 + lambda * (vT − v4)` without training. Sweep lambda and
   measure whether 9B immediately reduces detail while 32B improves it.
3. Split each teacher correction into low/high spatial-frequency components.
   Train or sample with one component removed.
4. Measure the cosine between the eventual LoRA checkpoint update and each
   teacher correction. This separates “bad teacher direction” from optimizer
   distortion.
5. Test a directional gate that suppresses corrections anti-aligned with
   x0 gradients/Laplacian. A magnitude-only P-OPD gate cannot distinguish the
   two teachers observed here.

## Full-capture VAE decode follow-up

The 16 stratified full-capture prompts were decoded at steps
`0,4,8,12,16,20,24,27` with the shared FLUX.2 4B VAE. For every model,
`x0 = x_t - (timestep / 1000) * model_output`. The 512px latent layout was
reconstructed with the FLUX.2 pipeline itself: `(1,1024,4)` IDs over a `32x32`
grid with `T=0`, row-major `H/W`, and `L=0`. A pipeline pack/unpack round trip
and exact cross-model `x_t` equality are required before decoding.

Outputs:

```text
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/full_x0/summary.json
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/full_x0/image_metrics_rows.csv
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/full_x0/images/
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/full_x0/grids/
.../teacher_gap_v1/analysis/full_x0/metric_curves.{png,pdf}
```

Across 128 prompt-step observations, with 95% CIs from 10,000 cluster-bootstrap
draws over the 16 prompts:

- 4B–9B: LPIPS `0.0614` `[0.0527, 0.0713]`, DINO cosine `0.9359`
  `[0.9264, 0.9457]`, CLIP cosine `0.9625` `[0.9522, 0.9714]`.
- 4B–32B: LPIPS `0.2672` `[0.2364, 0.2984]`, DINO cosine `0.7796`
  `[0.7532, 0.8079]`, CLIP cosine `0.8787` `[0.8580, 0.8959]`.
- Relative to 4B, 9B reduces decoded gradient energy by `6.90e-5`
  (`[-1.02e-4, -3.99e-5]`) and Laplacian variance by `4.50e-4`
  (`[-6.61e-4, -2.65e-4]`).
- Relative to 4B, 32B increases gradient energy by `6.07e-4`
  (`[3.15e-4, 9.56e-4]`) and Laplacian variance by `4.34e-3`
  (`[2.00e-3, 7.31e-3]`).
- FFT high-frequency *ratio* is not consistent with the derivative metrics:
  9B shift `+0.00460` `[-0.00098, 0.01204]`; 32B shift `-0.01103`
  `[-0.02813, 0.00108]`. Because this is normalized by total spectral power
  and early 32B predictions make large semantic/low-frequency changes, it
  should not be interpreted alone as sharpness.

The decoded result therefore reproduces the latent-space sign reversal:
9B stays perceptually close to 4B but removes local edge energy, while 32B makes
a much larger semantic/perceptual move and adds local gradient/Laplacian detail.
