\title{\textbf{OPD vs.\ DMD: the loss/gradient relationship}\\[0.3em]
\large Pointwise velocity regression vs.\ distribution matching for
flow-matching distillation, and why they coincide only in the infinite-capacity limit}
\author{Flow-Factory / XOPD notes}
\date{2026-07-27}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We make precise the relationship between the \emph{pointwise} distillation loss used by
DiffusionOPD / XOPD (velocity or clean-latent MSE, ``\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space}'') and the
\emph{distribution-matching} loss of DMD/DMD2. Writing both gradients in the shared flow-matching
$\xh$ parameterization, we show they carry the \emph{same} error signal $(\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi)$ but
differ in (i) the Jacobian they backpropagate through --- $\partial_\theta v_\theta$ (the velocity
output at a fixed input) for OPD vs.\ $\partial_\theta x$ (the generated sample, through the
sampler) for DMD --- and (ii) whether the ``fake'' term is an \emph{independent, online} score of
the student distribution. The two objectives share an optimum under infinite capacity, but under a
capacity gap the $L^2$-optimal field differs from the $\KL$-optimal distribution. A corollary: DMD
with the fake pinned to the teacher, or to the student's own one-step map, collapses to a
(reweighted) MSE; genuine distribution matching requires the third, online fake-score network.
This is exactly the piece the self-normalized \texttt{x0\_norm} space structurally lacks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and notation}
Rectified-flow / flow matching on a shared latent space. For a clean latent $x_0$, noise
$\varepsilon\sim\N(0,I)$, and schedule $\sigma=\sigma(t)=t/T\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
x_t=(1-\sigma)\,x_0+\sigma\,\varepsilon,\qquad
v=\varepsilon-x_0,\qquad
\xh(x_t)=x_t-\sigma\,v(x_t),
\end{equation}
so the velocity $v$ and the one-step clean estimate $\xh$ are interchangeable. The (frozen) teacher
velocity field is $v_\psi$, the student is $v_\theta$; write
$\xh^\theta=x_t-\sigma v_\theta$, $\xh^\psi=x_t-\sigma v_\psi$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resid}
\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi=-\sigma\,(v_\theta-v_\psi).
\end{equation}
The student induces a sample distribution $p_\theta$ (its ODE/consistency rollout); the teacher/target
is $p_{\real}$.

\section{OPD: pointwise field regression}
DiffusionOPD / XOPD's per-step term $d_k$ is a plain MSE in one of four spaces
(\texttt{xopd\_dk\_space}$\in\{v,\,xt,\,x0,\,x0\_norm\}$). Using \eqref{eq:resid}, at a
\emph{given} $x_t$,
\begin{align}
L^{(v)}   &=\E\norm{v_\theta-v_\psi}^2,
&\nabla_\theta L^{(v)}   &=2\,\E\!\big[(v_\theta-v_\psi)\,\partial_\theta v_\theta\big],\\
L^{(x_0)} &=\E\norm{\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi}^2=\E\,\sigma^2\norm{v_\theta-v_\psi}^2,
&\nabla_\theta L^{(x_0)} &=2\,\E\!\big[\sigma^2(v_\theta-v_\psi)\,\partial_\theta v_\theta\big],\\
L^{(xt)}  &=\E\norm{\mu_\theta-\mu_\psi}^2\propto\E\,\Delta t^2\norm{v_\theta-v_\psi}^2,
&\nabla_\theta L^{(xt)}  &=2\,\E\!\big[\Delta t^2(v_\theta-v_\psi)\,\partial_\theta v_\theta\big],
\end{align}
where $\mu=x_t+v\,\Delta t$ is the Euler transition mean. Thus the three ``geometric'' spaces are
one gradient reweighted by $a(t)\in\{1,\Delta t^2,\sigma^2\}$ (the identity
$\mathrm{MSE}(v):\mathrm{MSE}(xt):\mathrm{MSE}(x_0)=1:\Delta t^2:\sigma^2$). The
self-normalized space divides the $x_0$ loss by a detached per-sample scale,
\begin{equation}
L^{(x0\_norm)}=\E\!\left[\frac{\norm{\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi}^2}
{\sg\big(\overline{|\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi|}\big)+\epsilon}\right],
\qquad
\nabla_\theta L^{(x0\_norm)}=\E\!\left[\frac{2\sigma\,(v_\theta-v_\psi)\,\partial_\theta v_\theta}
{\sg(\text{scale})}\right],
\end{equation}
which equalizes each step's gradient magnitude (DiffusionNFT/DMD-style adaptive reweighting) but
does \emph{not} change the direction. \textbf{Two structural facts hold for every OPD variant:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item the \emph{target} is the teacher \emph{field} $v_\psi$ (equivalently $\xh^\psi$) evaluated at
the \emph{same} input $x_t$; the error signal is $(v_\theta-v_\psi)\propto(\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi)$;
\item the gradient backpropagates through $\partial_\theta v_\theta$ --- the velocity \emph{output}
at a \emph{fixed} input $x_t$. It is behaviour-cloning of a vector field; the sampler is never
differentiated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{DMD: distribution matching}
DMD trains a generator $x=G_\theta(z,c)$ (few-step) to minimize $\KL(p_\theta\,\|\,p_{\real})$ via
the variational-score-distillation gradient. With a Gaussian perturbation at level $t$, the score
and the one-step clean estimate are related by
$\nabla\log p_t(x_t)=-(x_t-\xh^{p})/\sigma^2$, so the score \emph{difference} between the target and
the student's own distribution is
\begin{equation}
s_{\real}(x_t)-s_{\fake}(x_t)\;\propto\;\frac{\xh^{\fake}(x_t)-\xh^{\real}(x_t)}{\sigma^2},
\end{equation}
where $\xh^{\real}$ comes from the frozen teacher and $\xh^{\fake}$ from an \emph{online} score
network trained (by denoising) to track the current $p_\theta$. The DMD gradient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmd}
\nabla_\theta L_{\mathrm{DMD}}
=\E_{t,\,x=G_\theta(z)}\!\Big[w(t)\,\big(s_{\fake}(x_t)-s_{\real}(x_t)\big)\;\partial_\theta x\Big].
\end{equation}
The DMD2 implementation realizes \eqref{eq:dmd} in $\xh$ space with a stop-gradient identity. With
$p_{\real}=x-\xh^{\real}$, $p_{\fake}=x-\xh^{\fake}$, and the self-normalization
$\mathrm{norm}=\overline{|p_{\real}|}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmd2}
g=\frac{p_{\real}-p_{\fake}}{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{\xh^{\fake}-\xh^{\real}}{\mathrm{norm}},
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{DMD}}=\tfrac12\norm{x-\sg(x-g)}^2
\;\Rightarrow\;
\nabla_\theta L_{\mathrm{DMD}}=g\,\partial_\theta x .
\end{equation}
\textbf{Two structural facts:} (i) the direction is the \emph{difference of two scores}
$\xh^{\fake}-\xh^{\real}$, moving $x$ \emph{away from} its own current density and \emph{toward} the
target; (ii) the gradient backpropagates through $\partial_\theta x$ --- the generated \emph{sample}
position, i.e.\ through the sampler.

Both gradients are ``(an $\xh$ residual) $\times$ (a Jacobian)''. Table~\ref{tab:cmp} lines them up.

\subsection{Differentiate w.r.t.\ $v$ or w.r.t.\ $x$}
This is the crux the two objectives disagree on. OPD moves $\theta$ so the velocity \emph{output}
$v_\theta(x_t)$ matches $v_\psi(x_t)$ at inputs $x_t$ drawn from the rollout, holding $x_t$ fixed in
the backward pass. DMD moves $\theta$ so the \emph{sample} $x=G_\theta(z)$ shifts along
$\xh^{\real}-\xh^{\fake}$, differentiating through the sampler ($\partial_\theta x$). Field-matching
vs.\ sample-moving.

\subsection{Reduction 1: fake $=$ teacher $\Rightarrow$ no signal}
If the online fake is pinned to the teacher, $\xh^{\fake}=\xh^{\real}$ and \eqref{eq:dmd2} gives
$g=0$: DMD is \emph{degenerate}. Note this is \emph{not} what $x0\_norm$ does: $x0\_norm$ keeps the
\emph{OPD} residual $\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi$ (student$-$teacher) and only borrows DMD2's per-sample
self-normalization. So
\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\ x0\_norm=\text{OPD }x_0\text{ residual }+\text{ DMD2 self-normalization};\ \text{it is
\emph{not} distribution matching (no online fake, differentiates }v\text{, not }x).\ }
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Reduction 2: fake $=$ the student's own one-step map $\Rightarrow$ reweighted MSE}
Suppose one ``cheaply'' sets $\xh^{\fake}=\xh^\theta$ (the student's own one-step clean prediction)
and $\xh^{\real}=\xh^\psi$. Then the DMD direction becomes the OPD residual,
\begin{equation}
g=\frac{\xh^{\fake}-\xh^{\real}}{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi}{\mathrm{norm}},
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:dmd2} gives $\nabla_\theta L=g\,\partial_\theta x$, versus OPD's
$\nabla_\theta L^{(x_0)}=(\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi)\,\partial_\theta\xh^\theta
=-(\xh^\theta-\xh^\psi)\,\sigma\,\partial_\theta v_\theta$. \emph{Same error signal, different
Jacobian} ($\partial_\theta x$ vs.\ $-\sigma\,\partial_\theta v_\theta$).

\begin{prop*}[No free lunch]
Replacing $\partial_\theta v_\theta$ by $\partial_\theta x$ (routing the same MSE residual through
the sampler), or pinning the fake to teacher/student, does \emph{not} produce a distribution loss.
Equation~\eqref{eq:dmd} equals the $\KL$ gradient only when $s_{\fake}=\nabla\log p_\theta$ is an
\emph{independent, freshly trained} estimate of the student's own (moving) score. With
fake$=$teacher the signal vanishes; with fake$=$student it is a biased, reweighted MSE routed
through the rollout. Hence genuine DMD is irreducibly a \emph{three-network} system
(real, online-fake, student); it cannot be obtained from OPD by an algebraic substitution.
\end{prop*}

\subsection{Why they agree only in the infinite-capacity limit}
If the student can represent the teacher field exactly, the optimum $v_\theta^\star=v_\psi$ makes
\emph{both} gradients vanish (OPD residual $=0$; then $p_\theta=p_{\real}$ so the DMD score
difference $=0$): the objectives share this optimum, and pointwise matching a \emph{deterministic}
teacher field is the principled thing to do (it is not the ``mode-averaging'' trap, because the
per-input target is single-valued). Under a capacity gap the objectives diverge: OPD returns the
$L^2$ projection onto the field (a conditional mean, whose \emph{induced} distribution can be
blurred), whereas DMD returns the $\KL$-closest distribution (mode-seeking, sharper). The $L^2$
metric on the velocity field is simply not the $\KL$ metric on the transported distribution, so for
a capacity-limited student (small model / few steps) DMD can produce better samples even when
distilling the very same deterministic teacher.

\section{Extensions (for completeness)}
\paragraph{TDM (Trajectory Distribution Matching).} Match $p_\theta$ to $p_{\real}$ not only at the
endpoint but at several trajectory times $t\in\{0.75,0.5,0.25,0\}$, so the gradient acts on the
whole latent sequence rather than the final sample; this constrains the transport \emph{dynamics},
not just the terminal density.
\paragraph{Transport-operator view.} A flow is an operator $\Phi_{1\to0}$. Merging teachers is a
mixture of induced distributions $\Phi_S\approx\sum_i\pi_i\Phi_i$ (a distribution-level statement),
and factorizing is a composition $\Phi_T\approx\Phi_K\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1$; the linear
velocity combination $v=\sum_i w_i v_i$ is only a first-order surrogate for these, which is why
velocity-space MSE and image/trajectory-space distribution matching are genuinely different.

\section{Practical implications for XOPD}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item OPD's move \texttt{xt}$\to$\texttt{v}/\texttt{x0} fixed the per-step reweighting (removing the
$\Delta t^2$ last-step dominance) and is stable and correct in the capacity limit; it is
\emph{not} ``going backwards''.
\item \texttt{x0\_norm} $=$ OPD residual $+$ DMD2 self-normalization: it equalizes per-step gradient
magnitude but is still pointwise field regression --- no online fake, differentiates $v$ not $x$.
\item To obtain a genuine distribution signal one must add the \emph{online fake-score} network and
differentiate through the sampler ($\partial_\theta x$), i.e.\ the \texttt{XDMDTrainer} design
(\texttt{docs/xopd/dmd\_cross\_model\_design.md}): real $=$ 32B teacher, fake $=$ a second LoRA
adapter tracking $p_\theta$, student $=$ the few-step generator; direction
$\xh^{\fake}-\xh^{\real}$, applied by the stop-gradient identity \eqref{eq:dmd2}. That online fake
is precisely the term \texttt{x0\_norm} lacks.
\end{itemize}
